    How it works

    What “last place” means

    It’s how far from the school the furthest child admitted on distance lived, measured the way the school measures it. It changes every year and is not a fixed boundary.

    How North Yorkshire offers places

    After looked-after children and approved medical or social cases, children living in the school’s catchment are offered places first, then children of staff, then children from outside the catchment. Within each group brothers and sisters of pupils come first, then whoever lives nearest. Each school’s year-by-year table puts that into words:

    • Everyone in the catchment, plus others within 1.22 mi: every child in the catchment got a place, and children outside it got one if they lived within 1.22 miles.
    • Only children in the catchment, within 0.27 mi: places ran out inside the catchment. Nobody outside it got in, and inside it only children within 0.27 miles did.
    • Everyone in the catchment, plus brothers and sisters of pupils: from outside the catchment, only siblings got in.
    • By the school’s own rules: academies, voluntary aided and foundation schools set their own rules, and faith schools may put church members first.

    Would you get in? compares that with where you live: whether you’re in the catchment, and how far you are from the school.

    The results

    Each school is judged across every year published unless you pick a single year. Safe means you’d have got in every year. Mixed means in some years but not others. Just inside means inside every year but by under 0.1 mile at least once, which is within the margin of error. Unlikely means outside every year; if every miss was under 0.1 mile it’s marked “Just outside” instead. Open means everyone who applied got a place, or the last out-of-catchment place went over 2 miles away. Years marked “Not published” don’t count either way. Sibling priority isn’t included, so if you already have a child at a school your chances there are better than shown.

    Catchments

    Your postcode’s centre point (or the exact point, if you use your location or drop a pin) is checked against the council’s catchment map, which the council publishes for reference only. If you live near a boundary, check your exact address on the council’s map. Open a school to see its catchment outlined on the map.

    Distances

    Most schools measure the shortest walking route along roads and footpaths, from your home to the nearest school entrance, using the council’s own mapping. Walking distances here use OpenStreetMap paths from your postcode centre to the school, so expect differences of 0.1 mile or more. Where a school doesn’t say how it measures, straight line is assumed. If the walking-route service can’t be reached, those schools are marked “Check”. On the map, walking-route schools show a dashed circle: the true walking area always sits inside it.

    Ofsted

    Ofsted no longer gives one overall grade. Schools inspected before September 2024 keep their old grade (Outstanding to Inadequate). Those inspected from September 2024 to November 2025 have area grades only. Since November 2025 schools get a report card rating six or seven areas from Exceptional to Urgent improvement. Short “ungraded” inspections only confirm whether a school is still at its previous grade.

    About the data
    • Reception applications and the last distance offered for 2023 to 2026 entry: North Yorkshire Council admissions statistics on Data North Yorkshire.
    • Catchment areas and school locations: the council’s school maps, updated September 2025.
    • Inspections: Ofsted management information as at 30 September 2026.
    • Key dates: the council’s primary admissions timetable for 2027 to 2028.

    Junior schools (Oatlands Junior, Saltergate Junior) take children into Year 3, so they aren’t included. Gathered October 2026. Always check the school’s current admissions policy and the council’s own figures before applying.
