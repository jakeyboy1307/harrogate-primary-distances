How to read this

“Last distance offered” is how far from the school the furthest child admitted on distance lived, measured the way the school measures it. It changes every year and is not a fixed boundary.

North Yorkshire puts catchment first. After looked-after children and approved medical or social cases, children living in the school’s catchment are offered places, then children of staff, then children from outside the catchment. Within each group siblings come first, then whoever lives nearest. So the council says which group the last place went to:

  • A plain distance, such as 1.22 mi, is the cut-off for children outside the catchment. Everyone in the catchment got a place.
  • In-catchment 0.27 mi means places ran out inside the catchment. Children outside it got none, and children inside it got in up to that distance.
  • Siblings only means everyone in the catchment got in, but from outside it only children with a brother or sister at the school did.
  • Academies, voluntary aided and foundation schools set their own rules. A few report the last place under a numbered criterion from their own policy, and faith schools may put church members first.

By default each school is judged across every year published. Safe means you’d have got in every year. Mixed means in some years but not others. Just inside means inside every year but by under 0.1 mile at least once, which is within the tool’s margin of error. Unlikely means outside every year; if every miss was under 0.1 mile it’s marked “Just outside” instead. Open means everyone who applied got a place, or the last out-of-catchment place went over 2 miles away, so nobody nearby missed out. Years marked “not published” are ones where the council told parents to ask the school, so they don’t count either way. Sibling priority isn’t modelled: if you already have a child at a school, your chances there are better than shown. Pick a single year from the menu to see that year alone.

The catchment check uses your postcode centre (or the exact point, if you use Locate me or drop a pin) against the council’s catchment map, which the council publishes for reference only. If you live near a boundary, check your exact address on the council’s map. Tap a school to see its catchment on the map.

Most schools measure the shortest walking route along roads and footpaths, from a fixed point in your home to the nearest school entrance, using the council’s own mapping. Walking distances here use OpenStreetMap footpaths from your postcode centre to the school’s location, so expect differences of 0.1 mile or more. Where a school doesn’t say how it measures, straight line is assumed. If the walking-route service can’t be reached, those schools are marked “Check”, as the straight line is shorter than any walk. On the map, walking-route schools show a dashed circle: the true walking area always sits inside it.

Ofsted no longer gives one overall grade. Schools inspected before September 2024 keep their old grade (Outstanding to Inadequate). Those inspected from September 2024 to November 2025 have area grades only. Since November 2025 schools get a report card rating six or seven areas (seven where there’s early years provision) from Exceptional to Urgent improvement. Short “ungraded” inspections only confirm whether a school is still at its previous grade. Ofsted data is as at 30 September 2026.

Junior schools (Oatlands Junior, Saltergate Junior) take children into Year 3, so they aren’t included.

Data: North Yorkshire Council school admissions statistics for 2023 to 2026 entry (Data North Yorkshire), the council’s catchment and school maps (September 2025), and Ofsted management information. Gathered October 2026. Always check the school’s current admissions policy and the council’s own figures before applying.
